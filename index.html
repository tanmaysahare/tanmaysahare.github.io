FLAGSHIP: WHAT I'M BUILDING

VarshaKrishi Weekly satellite crop advice

The problem

Most Indian smallholder farmers decide when to irrigate and what to check by habit, because no one looks at their individual field every week.

The approach

Free satellite imagery (Sentinel-2, NASA HLS, Sentinel-1) and weather, turned into one honest message a week on WhatsApp, in the farmer's own language, with the source, the date and the limits of every signal stated.

  • Satellite crop advice: NDVI/NDRE/EVI/NDWI per field, trend against the field's own history, delivered weekly on WhatsApp in Marathi, Hindi or English. Other Indian languages are machine-translated and labelled.
  • Watch and Decide: Watch is free for any field in India (trend, weather, honest "no reliable signal" states, no prescriptions). Decide is crop-specific and agronomist-reviewed, starting with Nagpur orange in Vidarbha for the 2026-27 season.
  • Provenance: every message shows its source, its observation date and its limits; derived records are hash-chained per field.
  • Cited agronomy: FAO-56 evapotranspiration irrigation models and literature-cited disease-risk forecasting (TOMCAST DSV).
  • Who pays: farmers can buy a Decide season pass, partners can sponsor farmer groups, programmes can pay per farmer-season. Watch stays free. No farmer price is published until a group has paid it.

About me

I'm the founder of VarshaKrishi, which sends Indian farmers weekly satellite crop advice about their own field on WhatsApp, in Marathi, Hindi and English. I'm an EiR Fellow at the 5G/6G Centre of Excellence, FIRST, IIT Kanpur, and a B.Tech Aerospace Engineering student at IIT Kanpur.

VarshaKrishi builds one product with two tiers. Watch is free for any field in India; Decide is crop-specific, reviewed by agronomists, and proven one crop and one belt at a time, starting with Nagpur orange in Vidarbha for the 2026-27 season.

It is built from the farmer's question backwards: what changed on my field, and what should I check this week? The stack today is a satellite imagery pipeline on free data, FastAPI backends, a Next.js dashboard for partners, WhatsApp delivery, and agronomy engines built from cited literature (FAO-56 evapotranspiration, TOMCAST disease models), with every message carrying its source, date and limits.

Before agritech, I built quantitative and data systems: CORE (an automated portfolio risk engine), a real-time sports-market arbitrage monitor, and TweetTracker (NLP policy sentiment analysis). That toolkit of models, data pipelines and shipped products is now pointed at Indian agriculture.

I'm looking for pilot partners, research collaborations and grant partnerships (KVKs, FPOs, agricultural universities, NGOs), and I'm always happy to talk engineering.
