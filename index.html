FLAGSHIP — WHAT I'M BUILDING

VarshaKrishi Agricultural Intelligence Infrastructure

The Problem

Most Indian smallholder farmers decide when to irrigate and what to check by habit, because no one looks at their individual field every week.

The Approach

Free satellite imagery (Sentinel-2, NASA HLS, Sentinel-1) and weather, turned into one honest message a week on WhatsApp, in the farmer's own language, with the source, the date and the limits of every signal stated.

  • Satellite crop advisory: NDVI/NDRE/EVI/NDWI per field, trend against a per-field baseline, delivered weekly on WhatsApp. Advice reviewed in Marathi and Hindi; other languages machine-translated and labelled.
  • Watch and Decide: Watch is free and national (trend, weather, honest "no reliable signal" states, no prescriptions). Decide is crop-specific and agronomist-reviewed, proven one belt at a time.
  • Provenance: every advisory shows its source, its observation date and its limits; derived records are hash-chained per field.
  • Science-grade advisories: FAO-56 evapotranspiration irrigation models and literature-cited disease-risk forecasting (TOMCAST DSV).
  • Who pays: farmers can buy a Decide season pass, partners can sponsor cohorts, programmes can pay per farmer-season. Watch stays free. No farmer price is published until a cohort has paid it.

    About Me

    I'm the founder of VarshaKrishi Udhyog Pvt. Ltd., an EiR Fellow, 5G/6G Centre of Excellence, FIRST, IIT Kanpur, and a B.Tech Aerospace Engineering student at IIT Kanpur. VarshaKrishi builds one product: satellite crop advice for Indian farmers, delivered weekly in the farmer's language on WhatsApp. Watch is free and national; Decide is crop-specific, reviewed by agronomists, and proven one crop and one belt at a time, starting with citrus in Vidarbha.

    I stopped building hardware in 2026 and started listening to farmers. The stack today is a satellite imagery pipeline on free data, FastAPI backends, a Next.js dashboard for partners, WhatsApp delivery, and agronomy engines built from cited literature (FAO-56 evapotranspiration, TOMCAST disease models), with every advisory carrying its source, date and limits.

    Before agritech consumed me, I built quantitative and data systems: CORE (an automated portfolio risk engine), a real-time sports-market arbitrage monitor, and TweetTracker (NLP policy sentiment analysis). That toolkit — models, data pipelines, shipped products — is now pointed at Indian agriculture.

    I'm looking for pilot partners, research collaborations and grant partnerships (KVKs, FPOs, agricultural universities, NGOs) — and I'm always happy to talk engineering.
